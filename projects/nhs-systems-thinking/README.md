# NHS Waiting Lists, Workforce Pressure & Patient Outcomes

**Domain:** systems thinking · system dynamics · healthcare operations

## Project framing
This project examines interactions between waiting-list pressure, workforce capacity, staff burnout, productivity, treatment throughput and policy response. Feedback, delays and unintended consequences make a systems approach useful.

## Feedback structures from project notes

### Reinforcing loop — capacity pressure
Waiting-list size increases staff burnout; burnout reduces staff productivity; lower productivity reduces treatment throughput; lower throughput increases the waiting list. This feedback can amplify pressure.

### Balancing loop — capacity expansion
Waiting-list pressure increases policy attention; policy attention may increase funding; funding may enable recruitment; more clinicians can increase treatment throughput; higher throughput can reduce waiting-list size. Funding and recruitment take time, and capacity does not translate instantly into completed treatments.

## Model scope
Coursework notes describe a waiting-list stock with referral inflow and treatment-completion outflow, clinician-linked capacity, and delays between policy pressure, funding and staffing. A diagnostic subsystem was also explored separately.

Numerical constants should be treated as illustrative unless supported by documented empirical evidence. Do not present them as measured NHS parameters without validation.

## Questions to investigate
- Which feedback loops dominate under different assumptions?
- How do funding and recruitment delays affect long-term behaviour?
- How sensitive are outcomes to referrals, capacity and productivity?
- Could an intervention shift pressure elsewhere in the pathway?
- What evidence is needed before applying the model to policy?
